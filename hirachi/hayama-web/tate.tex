Then $h_{n}$ is an eigenfunction of the harmonic oscillator $-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+x^{2}$ with the eigenvalue $\lambda_{n}$.}\bigskip\noindent {\bf Theorem: } \bLARGE{Let $0 < \varepsilon < \omega$, $0 < c$. \begin{enumerate}\item[{\bf (a)}] For $x=\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}\cos \phi$, $\epsilon < \phi <\pi -\epsilon$, \[\begin{split}|h_{n}(x)|^{2}&=\frac{1}{\pi \sqrt{\lambda_{n}}\sin \phi} \times \\&\left[\sin^{2}\left( \frac{\lambda_{n}}{4}(\sin(2\phi)-2\phi)+\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)+O(1/n),\right]\end{split}\]\item[{\bf (b)}] for $x=\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}\cosh \phi$, $\varepsilon \leq \phi \leq \omega$, \[|h_{n}(x)|^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{\lambda_{n}}\sinh \phi}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{n}(\sinh \phi -2\phi)}(1+O(1/n)),\] \item[{\bf (c)}] for $x=\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}-2^{-1/2}3^{-1/6}t$, $|t| \leq c$, \[|h_{n}(x)|^{2}=\left(\frac{6^{1/3}}{\pi}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{1/6}}(A(t)^{2} +O(n^{-2/3})),\]where $A(t)$ is the Airy function. \end{enumerate}}\noindent{\bf $\bullet$ Real and Complex eigenfunctions}\bLARGE{\begin{itemize}\item[(A)] Real eigenfunctions (Schr\"{o}dinger):  Eigenfunctions of elliptic operators $\varphi_{\lambda}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$ or $\varphi(\lambda(\hbar))$ with parameter $\hbar$ on a manifold $B$. Or their lift to the phase space $M=T^{*}B$: \begin{itemize}\item[(1)] Their microlocal lift\[C^{\infty}(T^{*}M) \ni a \mapsto \ispa{\op_{\hbar}(a)\varphi_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda}} \in \mb{C}. \]\item[(2)] On the phase space $\mb{R}^{2n}$, Wigner function \[\begin{split}(&W_{\hbar}\varphi_{\lambda,\hbar})(x,\xi) \\&=(2 \pi \hbar)^{-n}\int_{\mb{R}^{n}}e^{-i\eta \xi/\hbar}\varphi_{\lambda}(x+\tf{1}{2} \eta)\overline{\varphi_{\lambda,\hbar}(x-\tf{1}{2} \eta)}\,d\eta.\end{split}\]or Husimi function\[(H_{\hbar}\varphi_{\lambda})(x,\xi)=(W_{\hbar}\varphi * (\mbox{Gaussian}) )(x,\xi) \ (\geq 0).\]\end{itemize}\item[(B)] Complex Eigenfunction (Bargmann): \[\begin{array}{l}\bullet\  \mbox{phase space}=\mb{C}^{m}, \\\bullet\  \mbox{quantum state space}=H^{0}(\mb{C}^{m}) \\=\{f: \mbox{holom. on }\mb{C}^{m}\,;\,f \in L^{2}(\mb{C}^{m},e^{-|z|^{2}/2}dzd\bar{z})\}. \end{array}\]\noindent $\bullet$ Thus, the eigenfunctions on phase space should be in such a space as the Bargmann space. \medskip\noindent $\bullet$ Our complex eigenfunctions $\varphi_{\lambda}$ are on a `compact' complex phase space $(M,\omega)$ with a holomorphic Hermitian line bundle \[L \longrightarrow M \mbox{ with a Hermitian form }h \]such that $c_{1}(L)=\omega$. \bigskip\noindent $\bullet$ The eigenfunctions $\varphi_{\lambda} \in H^{0}(M,L^{\otimes N})$ ($N=1/(\mbox{Planck const.} \in \mb{N}$) would be eigenfunctions of automorphism(s) on $H^{0}(M,L^{\otimes N})$. \medskip\noindent $\bullet$ Analogue of the Husimi functions are of the form: $|\varphi_{N\lambda}(z)|^{2}_{h_{N}}$, $z \in M$where $h_{N}$ is a Hermitian metric on $L^{\otimes N}$ induced by $h$. \medskip\noindent $\bullet$ We are going to consider eigenfunctions for a completely integrable systems on a complex compact phase space. \end{itemize}}\noindent {\bf Why such eigenfunctions?}\bigskip\bLARGE{Usually, problems on asymptotics of eigenfunctions are quite hard to resolve completely. \bigskip$\Longrightarrow$ We need rather simple model for which one can completely determine the limit of distribution functions of eigenfunctions. \bigskip$\Longrightarrow$ There are a lot of non-trivial examples of completely integrable systems on compact complex phase spaces, and they are rather easy to handle.\bigskip$\Longrightarrow$ Toric manifolds and the ``toric'' eigenfunctions (sections) on it. }\newpage\begin{center}\bHuge{\S 2. Toric manifolds and ``toric eigenfunctions''}\end{center}\noindent \bLARGE{Let $(M,\omega)$ be a compact K\"{a}hler manifold (e.g., $\mb{C}P^{m}$). }\bigskip\noindent \bLARGE{$(M,\omega)$ is a toric manifold  \smallskip\noindent$\Longleftrightarrow$ $M$ is an algebraic variety with an action of the complex torus $(\mb{C}^{*})^{m}$ on $M$ which has a (Zariski) dense open orbit, and the $(\mb{C}^{*})^{m}$-action is an extension of the $(\mb{C}^{*})^{m}$ on $(\mb{C}^{*})^{m} \subset M$. \smallskip\noindent $\sim$ A certain completely integrable system on $(M,\omega)$. }\bigskip\noindent \bLARGE{$\bullet$ Completely integrable system is: } \medskip\bLARGE{$f_{1},\ldots,f_{m} \in C^{\infty}(M)$ ($\dim_{\mb{R}} M=2m$) such that \begin{itemize}\item $\{f_{i},f_{j}\}=0$, \item $df_{1},\ldots,df_{m}$ is linearly independent on (an open dense subset in) $M$. \end{itemize}\noindent $\Longleftrightarrow$ We have a (Hamiltonian) $\mb{R}^{m}$-action on $M$: $$\begin{gathered}t \cdot z :=\varphi_{t_{1}}^{1} \comp \cdots \comp \varphi_{t_{m}}^{m}(z),\qquadt=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}) \in \mb{R}^{m},\qquad z \in M \end{gathered}$$with the ``moment map'' $$\mu:M \to \mb{R}^{m},\qquad \mu(z)=(f_{1}(z),\ldots,f_{m}(z)). $$\bigskip\noindent $\bullet$ To obtain a toric manifold from the above completely integrable system on a compact K\"{a}hler manifold $(M^{2m},\omega)$, we need the following \bigskip\noindent $\bullet$ Requirement: \hspace{3pt} The Hamiltonian $\mb{R}^{m}$-action reduces to a global (effective) $T^{m}$-action on $M^{2m}$\bigskip\noindent $\bullet$ Fact (Delzant, Guillemin). $(M,\omega)$ with the above requirement has a structure of the toric manifold with the ``moment polytope''\[P:=\mu(M) \subset \mb{R}^{m}\]which is a polytope (convex hull of a finite set). }\bigskip\noindent $\bullet$ Toric eigenfunctions are eigenfunctions of a completely integrable system expressed by a (Hamiltonian) real torus action ${\bf T}^{m}$ on a compact K\"{a}hler toric manifold \[M_{P}=\mbox{phase space of real dimension } 2m\]with the moment polytope $P$. \bigskip\noindent $\bullet$ These are nothing but monomials, and which are realized as sections of a line bundle over a compact toric manifold: $$L_{P} \longrightarrow M_{P}. $$\noindent {$\bullet$ Fact: }\medskip\bLARGE{\noindent $\forall P \subset \mb{R}^{m}$ : integral Delzant polytope, \\$\exists (M_{P},\omega_{P})$ : a compact K\"{a}hler toric manifold,  $\dim_{\mb{R}}M_{P}=2m$, \\$\exists L_{P} \to M_{P}$ : a hol. line bundle with $c_{1}(L_{P})=\omega_{P}$ s.t., \\$\forall N (=1/\hbar) \in \mb{N}$, $H^{0}(M_{P},L_{P})$ is decomposed into the eigenspaces (weight spaces) of ${\bf T}^{m}$-action as follows: $$H^{0}(M_{P},L_{P}^{\otimes N}) =\bigoplus_{\gamma \in NP \cap \mb{Z}^{m}}\mb{C}\cdot \chi_{\gamma}^{P},$$where the eigenfunctions $\chi_{\gamma}^{P}$ are essentially the monomials on the open dense set $(\mb{C}^{*})^{m} \subset M_{P}$: $$\chi_{\gamma}^{P}(z)=z^{\gamma}=z_{1}^{\gamma_{1}} \cdots z_{m}^{\gamma_{m}},\quad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}) \in (\mb{C}^{*})^{m}.$$\noindent $\bullet$ The monomials $\chi_{\gamma}^{P}$ ($\gamma \in NP \cap \mb{Z}^{m}$) are orthogonal w.r.t. $$\ispa{s,t}_{N,P}=\int_{M_{P}}h_{N}(s(z),t(z))\,\omega_{P}^{m}/m!, $$given by the Hermitian structure. But they are not orthonormal. \medskip$\Longrightarrow$ The normalized eigenfunctions \[\varphi_{\gamma}^{P}:=\chi_{\gamma}^{P}/\|\chi_{\gamma}^{P}\|_{N,P}\]form an orthonormal basis for $H^{0}(M_{P},L_{P}^{\otimes N})$. $\Longrightarrow$ call it ``toric'' eigenfunctions.}\newpage\noindent {\bf $\bullet$ Basic Example}\bigskip\bLARGE{We shall show how to construct $M_{P}$ by an example. Let $m=1$, $P=[0,p]$ with $0 <p \in \mb{Z}$. Then, the monomial embedding\[\Phi_{P}:\mb{C}^{*} \ni z \mapsto [c_{0}:c_{1}z:\cdots:c_{p-1}z^{p-1}:c_{p}z^{p}] \in \mb{C}P^{p}\]is an embedding, where $c_{\alpha}={p \choose \alpha}^{1/2}$, the binomial coefficients. Then the Zariski closure \[M_{P}=\overline{\Phi_{P}(\mb{C}^{*})}^{{\rm Zariski}}\]is the image of the Veronese map: $$\Psi:\mb{C}P^{1}\ni [z_{0}:z_{1}] \mapsto [c_{0}z_{0}^{p}:c_{1}z_{0}^{p-1}z_{1}:\cdots : c_{p-1}z_{0}z_{1}^{p-1}:c_{p}z_{1}^{p}]\in \mb{C}P^{p}.$$Thus, we have $M_{P}=\mb{C}P^{1}$. The torus ${\rm T}^{1}=S^{1}$-action is given by \[e^{i\theta}[\zeta]=\Psi([z_{0}:e^{i\theta}z_{1}]),\quad [\zeta]=\Psi([z_{0}:z_{1}]) \in M_{P}. \]It is not hard to see that the K\"{a}hler form $\omega_{P}=\Psi^{*}\omega_{{\rm FS}p}$, where $\omega_{{\rm FS}p}$ is the Fubini-Study K\"{a}hler form on $\mb{C}P^{p}$, is given by $\omega_{P}=p\omega_{{\rm FS}1}$, where $\omega_{{\rm FS}1}$ is the Fubini-Study K\"{a}hler form on $\mb{C}P^{1}$. Hence $L_{P}:=\Psi^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)=\mathcal{O}(p)$, the $p$-th tensor power of the hyperplane line bundle over $\mb{C}P^{1}$. The moment map $\mu_{P}:M_{P}=\mb{C}P^{1} \to \mb{R}$ is given by \[\mu_{P}(\Psi[z_{0}:z_{1}])=\frac{p|z_{1}|^{2}}{|z_{0}|^{2}+|z_{1}|^{2}}.\]The weight space decomposition for $H^{0}(M_{P},L_{P}^{\otimes N})=H^{0}(\mb{C}P^{1},\mathcal{O}(Np))$ is given by \[\begin{gathered}H^{0}(\mb{C}P^{1},\mathcal{O}(Np))=\bigoplus_{\alpha=0}^{Np}\mb{C}\cdot \wh{\chi}_{\alpha},\\\wh{\chi}_{\alpha}([z_{0}:z_{1}])=z_{0}^{Np-\alpha}z_{1}^{\alpha},\quad \alpha \in [0,Np] \cap \mb{Z}. \end{gathered}\]Thus, on the open orbit $\mb{C}^{*}=\{[1:z] \in \mb{C}P^{1}\,;\,z \in \mb{C}^{*}\}$, the weight vector $\wh{\chi}_{\alpha}$ is identical to $\chi_{\alpha}(z)=z^{\alpha}$, $z \in \mb{C}^{*}$. }For $x \in P$ and $\alpha_{N} \in (NP) \cap \mb{Z}^{m}$, we call the sequence $\{\alpha_{N}\}_{N \geq 1}$ an {\it approximately multiple (AP)} of $x \in P$ if $$\alpha_{N}=Nx +O(1). $$(We regard the sequence $\{\alpha_{N}\}$ as a ``semiclassical ray'', which is, in a usual setting, $Nx$ when $x \in P \cap \mb{Z}^{m}$)\bigskip\noindent {\bf Our Problem is:}\bigskip\bLARGE{To find the asymptotic formula for the distribution function \[D_{\alpha_{N}}(t):=\vol_{M_{P}}(z \in M_{P}\,;\,|\varphi_{\alpha_{N}}^{P}(z)|_{N,P}^{2}>t),\quad t>0 \]for the toric eigenfunctions $\varphi_{\alpha_{N}}^{P}$. }\bigskip\noindent {\bf Related Works}\bLARGE{\begin{itemize}\item Kurlberg-Rudnick (mathematics) They obtains the limit distribution of the so-called ``Hecke eigenfunctions'' of a quantum cat map on the $2$-dim torus. Their eigenfunctions are that on the phase space. Their result is that the value distribution tends to the Wigner's semi-circle measure. \item Mirlin et al (physics) They considers a disordered system with a localization. They considers, in particular, a ``tail'' of eigenfunctions on a configuration space. Their results are universal, and they obtains ``logarithmic law'', which is quite similar to one of our result below. However, we consider rather ``center'' of the localization of eigenfunctions. \end{itemize}}\bigskip\begin{center}\bHuge{\S 3. Main Theorems}\end{center}\bLARGE{\noindent In what follows, we assume that $x \in P^{o}$.  }\bigskip\noindent {$\bullet$ \bf Un-rescaled distribution}\medskip\bLARGE{\begin{maintheo}Let $\alpha_{N} \in NP \cap \mb{Z}^{m}$ be an asymptotic multiple of $x \in P^{o}$. Then, we have $$D_{\alpha_{N}}(t) \sim \frac{(\pi m)^{m/2}}{c(P,x)\Gamma(m/2 +1)}\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right)^{m/2}, $$which does not depend on $t$, where the constant $c(P,x)$ is given by the constant $c(P,x)$ is $$c(P,x)=\lim_{N \to \infty}\left( \tf{N}{2\pi}\right)^{-m/2}\|\varphi_{\alpha_{N}}^{P}\|_{\infty}^{2}.$$\end{maintheo}}\medskip\bLARGE{Un-rescaled distribution functions have the universal limit, i.e., geometric data appear only in the constant $c(P,x)$. }\bigskip\noindent {$\bullet$ \bf Rescaled distribution:%\phantom{$\bullet$ }``Logarithmic law''}\bLARGE{\begin{maintheo}Define the rescaled distribution function$$F_{\alpha_{N}}(t)=\left (\tf{N}{2\pi}\right)^{m/2}\vol_{M_{P}}\left(z \in M_{P}\,;\,|\varphi_{\alpha_{N}}^{P}(z)|^{2}_{N} > (\tf{N}{2\pi})^{m/2}t\right)$$for $t > 0$. Then, we have $$\lim_{N \to \infty}F_{\alpha_{N}}(t)=\frac{1}{c(P,x)\Gamma(m/2 +1)}\left(\log \frac{c(P,x)}{t}\right)_{+}^{m/2},$$where the constant $c(P,x)$ is $$c(P,x)=\lim_{N \to \infty}\left( \tf{N}{2\pi}\right)^{-m/2}\|\varphi_{\alpha_{N}}^{P}\|_{\infty}^{2}.$$and $(\log s)_{+}$ denotes $\log s$ for $s \geq 1$ and $0$ otherwise. \end{maintheo}}\bLARGE{The rescaled distribution functions $F_{\alpha_{N}}(t)$ have also a universal limit, only what the constant $c(P,\alpha)$ and the power $m/2$ depend on the geometry of $M_{P}$. \bigskip$\Longrightarrow$ Natural question: Do the limits of distribution functions have any geometrical information?\bigskipAnswer is : YES. }\bigskip\noindent {\bf $\bullet$ Distribution functions with large length rescaling}\bLARGE{\begin{maintheo} We have: \[\begin{split}\lim_{N \to \infty}D_{\alpha_{N}}(e^{-Nt}) &=\vol_{M_{P}}(z \in (\mb{C}^{*})^{m}\,;\,b_{x}^{P}(z) <\log t) \\&= \int_{\{\rho \in \mb{R}^{m}\,;\, b_{x}^{P}(\rho) <t \}}\det A(\rho)\,d\rho,\end{split}\]where, $A(\rho)$ is a real positive $(m \times m)$-matrix valued smooth function such that \[c(P,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det A(\rho_{x})}}\]with the vector $\rho_{x}=\bar{\mu}_{P}^{-1}(x) \in \mb{R}^{m}$.  The function $b_{x}^{P}$ is a function on $(\mb{C}^{*})^{m}$  invariant under ${\bf T}^{m}$-action satisfying \[\partial_{\rho}b_{x}^{P}(\rho)=\bar{\mu}_{P}(\rho)-x, \quad b_{x}^{P}(\rho_{x}) =0\]For the map $\bar{\mu}_{P}$, see below. \end{maintheo}}\bigskip\noindent {\bf $\bullet$ Remarks}\bLARGE{\begin{itemize}\item The function $b_{x}^{P}$ is defined on$(\mb{C}^{*})^{m}/{\bf T}^{m} \cong \mb{R}^{m}$. The moment map induces the diffeomorphism: $$\bar{\mu}_{P}:\mb{R}^{m} \longrightarrow P^{o}, $$$b_{x}^{P}$ has a unique critical point $\rho_{x}=\bar{\mu}_{P}^{-1}(x)$.\item The function $b_{x}^{P}$ can not be extended to whole of $M_{P}$. \item The matrix-valued function $A(\rho)$, $\rho \in \mb{R}^{m}$ is given by $$A(\rho)=\partial_{\rho}\bar{\mu}_{P}(\rho),$$which can be shown to be positive definite for all $\rho \in \mb{R}^{m}$. \item The function $b_{x}^{P}$ looks like a quadratic form. In fact, we have$$b_{x}^{P}(\rho_{x}+u)=\ispa{A(\rho_{x})u,u}/2 +R_{3}(u). $$\end{itemize}}\begin{center}\bHuge{\S 3. Explanation of the ``logarithmic~law''}\end{center}\noindent {\bf $\bullet$ Pointwise asymptotics}\medskip\noindent \bLARGE{Let $\alpha_{N} \in NP \cap \mb{Z}^{m}$ be an (AP) of $x \in P^{o}$. Then we have \[|\varphi_{\alpha_{N}}^{P}(z)|_{N}^{2}=c(P,x)\left(\frac{N}{2\pi}\right)^{m/2}e^{-Nb_{x}^{P}(z)}(1+O(N^{-1}))\]locally uniformly on $(\mb{C}^{*})^{m} \subset M_{P}$, where\[c(P,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det A(\rho_{x})}}.\]\noindent $\bullet$ By the Taylor expansion for the function $b_{x}^{P}$, we have \[\begin{gathered}\left(\frac{N}{2\pi}\right)^{-m/2}|\varphi_{\alpha_{N}}^{P}(z)|_{N}^{2}=\frac{e^{-\ispa{A(\rho_{x})u,u}}/2}{\sqrt{\det A(\rho_{x})}}(1+O(N^{-1/2})),\\[10pt]z=e^{\rho_{x}+u/\sqrt{N}}\end{gathered}\]on a ball of radius $O(N^{-1/2})$ uniformly in $|u| \leq c$.}\bigskip\noindent {\bf $\bullet$ A Reason for the logarithmic law}\medskip\bLARGE{Concerning the above theorem, we consider the following Gaussian function\[g(u):=\frac{e^{-\ispa{Au,u}/2}}{\sqrt{\det A}},\quad u \in \mb{R}^{m},\]where $A$ is a real positive $(m \times m)$-matrix. Also consider the measure $\nu_{A}$ on $\mb{R}^{m}$ defined by \[\nu_{A}:=\frac{\det A}{(2\pi)^{m/2}}\,du\]The measure $\nu_{A}$ is not a finite measure, but we have \[\|g\|_{L^{k}(\nu_{A})}=\frac{1}{k^{m/2}(\det A)^{(k-1)/2}},\quad k=1,2,\ldots.\]Then, a direct computation shows: \begin{gather*}\nu_{A}(u \in \mb{R}^{m}\,;\,g(u)>t)=\frac{1}{c\Gamma(m/2 +1)}\left(\log \frac{c}{t}\right)^{m/2}\\[5pt]c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det A}},\quad 0 \leq t \leq c, \end{gather*}which is exactly the same form in one of our main theorems. \medskip$\Longrightarrow$ Thus we can say that:\begin{center}Monomials look like Gaussian around the corresponding invariant torus, and which makes the limit distribution obey the ``logarithmic law.'' \end{center}}\end{document}
