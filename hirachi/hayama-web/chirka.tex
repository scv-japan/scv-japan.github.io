graphs$S_\tau\sub\OM\times\CC\,$.One has to extend this family to a holomorphic foliation of the whole$\OM\times\CC\,$, i.e., so that the (holomorphic) graphs of the extendedfamily fill $\OM\times\CC\,$ and were mutually disjoint as well.This problem of the extension of "holomorphic motion"was positively solved by Slodkowski \cite{S} for the unit disc$\OM=\DD\sub\CC\,$ (for which it was originally formulated in\cite{MSS}).His proof is based on his description of polynomially convex hulls forcompact sets in $\pa\DD\times\CC\,$ with connected fibres(over the points of $\pa\DD\,$) and thus it does not valid for moregeneral bases $\OM$.In this paper we study the problem in case when$\OM$ is an arbitrary Riemann surface.Our approach, using quasi-linear $\bar\pa$-equations, gives,in particular, rather simple proof of the Slodkowski' theorem, validmoreover at once in an arbitrary plane domain.Conditions of the problem can be normed as follows.Fix (arbitrary) a base point $z_0\in\OM$.As the values $f_\tau(z_0)\in\CC\,$ are different for different$\tau$, one can take these numbers as indeces (instead of$\tau$), and so we assume in what follows that$f_\tau(z_0)=\tau\in E\sub\CC\,$.With variation of $z$, every point $\tau\in E$ "moves holomorphically" over$\OM$, i.e., $(z,f_\tau(z))$ varies along the holomorphic graph$S_\tau$.Mark in $E$ two points, $\tau_0\not=\tau_1$.The linear in $w$ transformation $(z,w)\mapsto(z,(w-f_{\tau_0})/(f_{\tau_1}-f_{\tau_0}))$ sends $f_{\tau_0},\,f_{\tau_1}$ to constant functions $0,1$.Holomorphic motion$(z,\tau)\mapsto f_\tau(z)$ with such marked sections($0,1\in E,\ f_0\equiv 0, f_1\equiv 1$) is called normed.As it is well-known, the family of all holomorphic maps$\OM\to\CC\sm\{0,1\}$ is normal, i.e., any sequence in this familycontains a subsequence converging uniformly on compact subsets of$\OM$ to a holomorphic function or to identical $\infty$.Thus, the problem of extension of holomorphic motion is sufficient to solveby additional assumptions that the set $E$ is finite, $\OM$ is a domainwith compact closure and smooth (or even $\RR$-analytic) boundary ina complex manifold $\tilde\OM$, and all the functions $f_\tau$ areholomorphic in $\tilde\OM$ (see \cite{MSS}, \cite{ST}).If $\OM$ is not simply-connected, there is a natural topologicalobstruction for the extension.Namely, assume that $\{f_\tau\}_{\tau\in E}$ is a normed holomorphic motionand $\{\tilde f_\tau\}_{\tau\in\CC\,}$ is a holomorphic motion over$\OM$ such that $\tilde f_\tau=f_\tau$ for all $\tau\in E$.If $\tau\not=\tau'$, then $\tilde f_\tau-\tilde f_{\tau'}$ does not vanishanywhere in $\OM$, and for any closed curve $\ga\sub\OM$ the variation ofargument $\Delta_\ga arg\,(\tilde f_\tau-\tildef_{\tau'})\in 2\pi i\,\ZZ$ is well-defined.This number depends continuously in$(\tau,\tau')\in\CC^2\sm\{\tau=\tau'\}$, hence, it must to be constantfor all $\tau\not=\tau'$.Thus we obtain a {\it necessary condition} of the existence ofholomorphic extension for normed holomorphic motion:\medpagebreak \np $(*)$ $\ \ \Delta_\ga\,arg\,(f_\tau-f_{\tau'})=0,\ \ \tau\not=\tau'\ $ {\it in\,} $E, \ $ {\it for any closed curve\/}$\ga\sub\OM$.\medpagebreak The main result is the following.\proclaim{Theorem} Let $\OM$ be an arbitrary Riemann surface and $\{f_\tau\}_{\tau\in E}$ be a normed holomorphic motion of a set $E\sub\CC\,$ over $\OM$ satisfying the necessary topological condition $(*)$.Then $\{f_\tau\}_{\tau\in E}$ extends to a holomorphic motion of thewhole plane $\CC\,$, i.e., there exists a family of holomorphic functions$\{\tilde f_\tau\}_{\tau\in\CC\,}$ in $\OM$ with mutually disjoint graphsfilling $\OM\times\CC\,$, such that $\tilde f_\tau=f_\tau$ for all$\tau\in E$.\endproclaimIt is not difficult to deduce from the condition $(*)$ the possibility ofextension of $\{f_\tau\}_{\tau\in E}$ in the class of continuous motionsover $\OM$, i.e., the existence of a family$\{\tilde f_\tau\}_{\tau\in\CC\,}$ of continuous functions in $\OM$ which graphs constitute a continuous foliation of $\OM\times\CC\,$ including the graphs of all $f_\tau,\ \tau\in E$(see Lemma).Thus, the Theorem can be reformulated as "Oka's principle" for holomorphicmotions (over a Riemann surface $\OM$):\smallpagebreak\np{\it If the problem of extension of given holomorphic motion over $\OM$ is solvable in the class of continuous motions then it is solvableas well in the class of holomorphic motions\/}.\smallpagebreakAs noted above, it is enough to prove the theorem by the followingadditional assumptions:$\OM$ is a domain with compact closure, smooth boundary and marked point$z_0\in\OM$ on a Riemann surface $\SS$ which we will assume further tobe {\it compact} (taking as $\SS$, for instance, the duble of $\OM$);$E$ is a finite subset of $\CC\,$;the functions $f_\tau$ are holomorphic in a \nbh of$\bar\OM$ on $\SS$.\bigpagebreak \flushpar {\bf 2.} In this section we prove the theoremfor the case when $\OM$ is a domain on the Riemann sphere $\SS=\hat\CC\,$,assuming for convenience that $z_0=\infty\in\OM$.To demonstrate the method of reduction of the problem to solution of aquasi-linear $\bar\pa$-equation, we consider first the case of the disc,more precisely, $\OM=\hat\CC\sm\bar\DD$ where $\DD$ is the unit disc.Extend $f_\tau,\ \tau\in E,$ onto the whole Riemann spheresetting $f(z)=f(1/\bar z),\ z\in\bar\DD$.These are continuous functions with mutually disjoint graphs, i.e.,$|f_\tau(z)-f_{\tau'}(z)|\ge\delta$ for all $z\in\hat\CC,\ \tau\not=\tau'$and some $\delta>0$.Let $\la(t)$ be a smooth function of $t\in\RR\,$, $\la(0)=1$ and $\la(t)=0$for $t>\delta /2$.The function $\phi(z,w)=\sum_{\tau\in E}\la(|w-f_\tau(z)|)(\paf_\tau/\pa\bar z)(z)$ is uniformly bounded in $\hat\CC\times\CC\,$(the first type discontinuities are possible on $\pa\DD\times\CC\,$), itvanishes outside of $\bar\DD\times\CC\,$ and satisfies a Lipschitz conditionin $w$-variable with the same constant $M$ for all $z\in\hat\CC\sm\pa\DD$.By the construction, every $f_\tau,\ \tau\in E,$ satisfies the quasi-linearequation$$\pa f/\pa\bar z =\phi (\,\cdot\,, f),\qquad f(\infty)=\tau$$(the derivative in sense of distributions).This equation is studied ditaily in \cite{CR}, and shortly the solution isas follows.For $f\in C(\hat\CC)$ the equation is equivalent to the integral one,$$f=\tau+Kf,\quad \text{where}\quad(Kf)(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_\DD\frac{\phi(\zeta,f(\zeta))}{\zeta-z}\,dS_\zeta.$$The operator $K$ in the space $C(\hat\CC)$ has uniformly bounded image(because $\phi$ is uniformly bounded)and is compact, because it sends continuous functions to the functions ofH\"older class with exponent$\al<1$ (arbitrary), and the H\"older norm for $Kf$ does not exceed$C(\al)\,max |f|$ (see, e.g., \cite{V}).By the Schauder fixed point Theorem,for any $\tau\in\CC\,$ there exists a function $f\in C^\al(\hat\CC)$such that $f=\tau+Kf$; that is a sought solution.This solution is unique and {\it holomorphic} in $\hat\CC\sm\bar\DD\,$;we denote it by $\tilde f_\tau\,$.Uniqueness, the crucial property of the equation, is a corollary ofthe Lipschitz condition for $\phi$ and the well-known principle:if $h\in C(\hat\CC),\ h(a)=0,\ \pa h/\pa\bar z\in L^\infty (\CC)$ and$|\pa h/\pa\bar z|\le M\,|h|$ pointwise almost everywhere, then$h\equiv 0$ (see, e.g., \cite{CR}).Here $a$ is an arbitrary given point in $\hat\CC\,$, hence, the graphsof $\tilde f_\tau$ are disjoint for different $\tau$.Thus, $\{\tilde f_\tau |_\OM\}_{\tau\in\CC}$ is a sought holomorphicextension of the holomorphic motion$\{f_\tau\}_{\tau\in E}$ over $\OM=\hat\CC\sm\bar\DD$.\medskipConsider now the case when $\OM$ is an arbitrary domain with smooth($C^\infty$) boundary on the Riemann sphere $\hat\CC\,$.The complement to $\OM$ consists of finite number of closed domainsdiffeomorphic to the disc.The extension of $f_\tau$ to $\hat\CC\,$, described above, is not valid ingeneral case, but the necessary condition $(*)$ helps in the case and asought extension is obtained from the following lemma.\proclaim{Lemma} Let the functions $g_0\equiv 0, g_1,...,g_N\inC^\infty(\pa\DD)$ have mutually disjoint graphsand $\Delta_{\pa\DD}\,arg\,(g_j-g_k) =0,\ j\not=k$.Then there exists a diffeomorphism of the cylinder$\bar\DD\times\CC$ of the form $(z,w)\mapsto(z,\psi(z,w))$ such that $\psi(z,g_j(z))\equiv g_j(1)$ are pairwisedifferent constants.In particular, there exist smooth extensions of the functions$g_j,\,j=0,...,N,$ to $\bar\DD$ such that their graphs over$\bar\DD$ are mutually disjoint.\endproclaim{\it The proof\/} proceeds by induction in $N$.Let one has made already a diffeomorphism setting$g_j,\ j<N,$ into constants $g_j(1)$.By the condition, the closed curve $g_N(\zeta),\ \zeta\in\pa\DD\,,$ haszero index with respect to any point$g_j(1),\ j<N,$ hence, there is a smooth homotopy$g_N(\zeta,r),\ 0\le r\le 1,$ such that $g_N(\cdot\,, 1)=g_N,\g_N(\cdot\,,r)\equiv g_N(1)$ for $r<1/2$ and $g_N(\zeta,r)$ doesnot accept any value $g_j(1),\ j<N$.Extend $g_N$ to $\bar\DD$ by the formula $g_N(z)=g_N(z/|z|,|z|)$ for$z\not=0,\ g_N(0):=g_N(1)$.Construct a smooth vector field $(z,v(z,w))$ in $\bar\DD\times\CC$, tangentto all (mutually disjoit) graphs of so extended$g_j,\ j=0,...,N,$ and such that $v(z,w)=0$ for$|z|<1/2$ and large $|w|$.The trajectories of this field starting from the points$(0,w)$ define a smooth retraction$\Psi:\bar\DD\times\CC\rightarrow 0\times\CC\,$ (trajectory throughthe point $(z,w)$ is sent to its beginning $(0,\psi(z,w))$).As the field is tangent to the graphs of$g_j$ they are sent by such retraction to $(0,g_j(0)=g_j(1))$.Hence, the map $(z,w)\mapsto (z,\psi (z,w))$ is a sought diffeomorphism.For $z$ fixed, denote $\psi(z,w)=:\psi_z(w)$.By the construction, the smooth functions$g_j(z):=(\psi_z)^{-1}(g_j(1))$ have mutually disjoint graphs in$\bar\DD$ and coinside with initial one on $\pa\DD$.$\quad\square$\smallpagebreakAfter such an extension we construct, as it was done above, the function$\phi(z,w)$ vanishing in $\OM\times\CC\,$, solve in the same way theequation $\pa f/\pa\bar z=\phi(\,\cdot\,,f),\ f(\infty)=\tau,\,$ and obtaina piece-wise foliation of $\hat\CC\times\CC\,$ consisting of thegraphs of there solutions; it gives over the domain $\OM\,$ the soughtholomorphic extension of given holomorphic motion.According to Sect.1, it proves Theorem for arbitrary plane domain$\OM$.\bigpagebreak \flushpar {\bf 3.} In the case of a Riemann surface$\OM$ of the genus $>0$ the problem appears to be essentially moredifficult.As noted in Sect.1, we can assume that$\OM$ is a domain with smooth boundary on a compact Riemann surface$\SS$ and that the set $E$ is finite.As $\SS$ we take the duble of $\OM\,$; then the motion of$E$ over $\OM$ extends by symmetry to a piece-wise smooth motion of$E$ over the whole $\SS$.By it, we can write down, as above,  suitable$\bar\pa$-equation, but even a linear$\bar\pa$-equation on a compact Riemann surface of genus$g\ge 1$ is not solvable in general, and the scheme presented abovedoes not work.One can not obtain a sought extension solving some special$\bar\pa$-equation, and we proceed in another way.First, extend the initial motion (already over $\SS$) to apiece-wise smooth motion of the whole plane$\CC\,$.By this motion-foliation write down $\bar\pa$-equation satisfying bythe functions with correspondent graphs (thus, we obtain an equationwhich is surely solvable).After that we start to variate the equation in the class of solvable one,decreasing to zero in $\OM$ the right-hand side of the equation.One can not variate in such a way all the graphs simultaneously, butit can be done for any fixed $\tau\in\CC\,$ using the implicit functiontheorem (in corresponding Banach space).Uniform estimates in the implicit function theorem give eventually thevariations of the whole foliation, and in limit they give a piece-wisesmooth foliation of $\SS\times\CC$ which is holomorphic over $\OM$ andsolves the original problem of holomorphic extension.The details of the program will be published later.\vskip5 mm\Refs\widestnumber\key{MSS}\ref\key{CR  }\by  {  }Chirka E., Rosay J.-P.\paper  Remarks on the proof of a generalized
